\begin{abstract}
Automated foliar disease identification in litchi (\textit{Litchi chinensis}) orchards is critical for food security and yield protection in South Asia. However, field-acquired imagery presents severe domain challenges, including severe background clutter, uncontrolled illumination, camera motion blur, and subtle inter-class visual discrepancies between fungal and algal leaf pathologies. Conventional deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers rely excessively on contextual background cues and suffer notable accuracy degradation under real-world agricultural corruptions. In this paper, we propose \textbf{LitchiHybridNet}, an edge-efficient dual-branch architecture that combines deep semantic representations from a lightweight MobileNetV3 backbone with high-frequency spatial-frequency representations extracted by a multi-scale, multi-orientation \textbf{Learnable Gabor Convolutional Bank} (24 channels, 8 orientations, 3 radial frequencies). A dedicated \textbf{Gabor Attention Fusion Module (GAFM)} dynamically balances spatial lesion boundaries and textural energy distributions via channel-wise cross-gating. To eliminate data leakage, we perform a rigorous audit of the BDLitchi dataset (11,094 images across 11 disease categories) and establish a group-aware, duplicate-free 70/15/15 stratified partition. Extensive multi-seed experiments demonstrate that LitchiHybridNet achieves a state-of-the-art Top-1 accuracy of \textbf{99.04\% $\pm$ 0.12\%} and a Macro-F1 score of \textbf{0.9904}, outperforming standard ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B0, MobileNetV3, and Swin-Transformer baselines with statistical significance ($p < 0.001$, McNemar and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests). Under severity-5 field motion blur and lighting corruption, LitchiHybridNet preserves a +12.6\% accuracy margin over pure CNN baselines. Finally, INT8 post-training quantization yields an ultra-lightweight 5.40\,MB model with 14.8\,ms inference latency on commodity x86 CPUs and 34.2\,ms on Raspberry Pi 4 edge hardware, demonstrating direct suitability for mobile precision farming.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{L}{itchi} (\textit{Litchi chinensis} Sonn.) is a high-value subtropical fruit crop cultivated extensively across Bangladesh, India, China, and Southeast Asia \cite{litchi_dataset_2022}. In Bangladesh, major production hubs such as Dinajpur and Ishwardi suffer annual harvest losses exceeding 25\% to 35\% due to destructive foliar pathogens, notably Anthracnose, Algal Leaf Spot, Leaf Blight, and Leaf Miner infestations \cite{islam2021survey}. Traditional manual visual inspection by agricultural extension officers is labor-intensive, subjective, and difficult to scale across millions of smallholder orchards.

Recent advances in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have shown remarkable progress in automated plant phenotyping \cite{mohanty2016using, ferentinos2018deep, barbedo2019plant}. However, the majority of existing models are evaluated on laboratory datasets captured against uniform backgrounds (e.g., PlantVillage \cite{hughes2015open}). When deployed in natural orchard environments, standard CNNs encounter severe practical limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Frequency Texture Ambiguity:} Pathological manifestations such as Algal Leaf Spot (\textit{Cephaleuros virescens}) and Red Rust produce granular, velvety spore patterns that share identical coarse color profiles but diverge sharply in spatial-frequency orientation. Standard convolutional kernels often fail to isolate these micro-textural signatures in early disease stages \cite{wang2021gabor}.
    \item \textbf{Environmental Clutter and Overfitting:} Real field images encompass soil, tree bark, weeds, and overlapping foliage. Deep networks tend to memorize extraneous background correlations rather than genuine lesion morphology \cite{singh2020deep}.
    \item \textbf{Resource Constraints at the Rural Edge:} Modern vision transformers (e.g., Swin-T \cite{liu2021swin}) demand tens of millions of parameters and gigabytes of memory, rendering them impractical for low-power handheld edge devices and smartphones used by local growers \cite{ahmed2022field}.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these fundamental challenges, we introduce \textbf{LitchiHybridNet}, a novel dual-branch hybrid architecture specifically tailored for field-condition litchi disease detection. The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Learnable Gabor Convolutional Filter Bank:} We design a 24-channel Gabor layer wherein orientation ($\theta$), radial frequency ($F$), phase ($\psi$), and aspect ratio ($\gamma$) are directly optimized via backpropagation, initialized from 2D Fourier power spectral density (PSD) analysis of leaf lesions.
    \item \textbf{Gabor Attention Fusion Module (GAFM):} We formulate a cross-attention gating mechanism that dynamically modulates deep MobileNetV3 semantic features with high-frequency Gabor spatial filters, suppressing background noise and reinforcing lesion boundary sharpness.
    \item \textbf{Leakage-Free Benchmark Split:} We conduct a comprehensive perceptual hash audit of the BDLitchi dataset (11,094 images across 11 classes), identifying 924 near-duplicate groups and establishing a verified, group-aware 70/15/15 train/val/test partition with zero train-test data leakage.
    \item \textbf{Extensive Empirical Validation:} Across 5 random seeds, LitchiHybridNet achieves 99.04\% accuracy and 0.9904 Macro-F1. In Hendrycks corruption stress-testing, our model exhibits a +12.6\% retention advantage over standard CNNs under severe blur.
    \item \textbf{Quantized Edge Deployment:} We deploy the model on ONNX Runtime with INT8 quantization, demonstrating 14.8\,ms latency on CPU and 34.2\,ms on a Raspberry Pi 4 edge node at only 5.40\,MB model size.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Deep Learning for Foliar Plant Pathology}
Automated crop disease recognition has evolved rapidly from hand-crafted color histograms to deep representations. Mohanty \textit{et al.} \cite{mohanty2016using} and Ferentinos \cite{ferentinos2018deep} demonstrated $>99\%$ classification accuracy on laboratory datasets. However, Barbedo \cite{barbedo2019plant} demonstrated that lab-trained models suffer accuracy drops exceeding 30\% when applied to field conditions due to canopy clutter and illumination shifts. Recent studies have attempted transfer learning with MobileNetV2 \cite{sandler2018mobilenetv2}, EfficientNet \cite{tan2019efficientnet}, and Vision Transformers \cite{dosovitskiy2020image, liu2021swin}, yet these architectures lack inductive spatial-frequency biases.

\subsection{Gabor Filters in Computer Vision}
Biologically inspired by 2D receptive fields in mammalian visual cortex \cite{daugman1985uncertainty}, Gabor filters provide optimal simultaneous localization in spatial and frequency domains \cite{fogel1989gabor, manjunath1996texture}. While classical agronomic systems relied on fixed, handcrafted Gabor banks \cite{kamencay2020improved}, Luan \textit{et al.} \cite{luan2018gabor} introduced Gabor Convolutional Networks for object detection. In this work, we advance this paradigm by introducing fully learnable continuous parameterization with adaptive attention fusion for plant pathology.

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\subsection{Learnable Gabor Filter Formulation}
A 2D spatial Gabor kernel $G(x, y; \theta, F, \sigma, \gamma, \psi)$ is defined as the product of an elliptical Gaussian envelope and a sinusoidal planar wave:
\begin{equation}
    G(x, y) = \exp\left(-\frac{x'^2 + \gamma^2 y'^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \cos(2\pi F x' + \psi)
\end{equation}
where the rotated coordinates $(x', y')$ are defined by:
\begin{equation}
    x' = x \cos\theta + y \sin\theta, \quad y' = -x \sin\theta + y \cos\theta
\end{equation}
Rather than treating $\{\theta, F, \sigma, \gamma\}$ as fixed hyperparameters, our formulation implements them as differentiable parameters optimized via stochastic gradient descent:
\begin{equation}
    \theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta}, \quad F \leftarrow F - \eta \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial F}
\end{equation}
We configure a 24-channel bank initialized across 8 uniform orientations $\theta_k = \frac{k\pi}{8}$ ($k \in \{0, \dots, 7\}$) and 3 radial frequencies $F \in \{0.05, 0.15, 0.25\}\,\text{cycles/pixel}$.

\subsection{LitchiHybridNet Architecture}
The overall network comprises two parallel feature extraction streams:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Texture Stream:} The input image $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224}$ is processed by the Learnable Gabor Bank ($24$ filters of kernel size $7 \times 7$), followed by spatial batch normalization, ReLU activation, and a $1 \times 1$ projection layer yielding texture representation $\mathbf{F}_{\text{gabor}} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 112 \times 112}$.
    \item \textbf{Deep Semantic Stream:} Concurrently, $\mathbf{X}$ is fed into a lightweight MobileNetV3-Large backbone \cite{howard2019searching} pre-trained on ImageNet \cite{krizhevsky2012imagenet}, extracting hierarchical feature maps $\mathbf{F}_{\text{cnn}} \in \mathbb{R}^{960 \times 7 \times 7}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Gabor Attention Fusion Module (GAFM)}
To effectively fuse multi-scale textural and semantic cues, GAFM applies a Squeeze-and-Excitation gating mechanism \cite{hu2018squeeze}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z} = \text{GAP}(\mathbf{F}_{\text{cnn}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{960}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{s} = \sigma\left(\mathbf{W}_2 \delta(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{z})\right)
\end{equation}
where $\delta$ denotes ReLU, $\sigma$ denotes the sigmoid activation, $\mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{\frac{C}{16} \times C}$, and $\mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times \frac{C}{16}}$. The refined fused feature vector $\mathbf{F}_{\text{out}}$ is computed via residual aggregation and fed into an MLP classification head with 11 class logits.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\subsection{Dataset and Group-Aware Partitioning}
The BDLitchi dataset contains 11,094 field-captured images categorized into 11 classes: Algal Leaf Spot, Anthracnose, Brown Rot, Canker, Die Back, Healthy Leaf, Leaf Gall Midge, Leaf Miner, Red Rust, Rust, and Yellow Mottle. We computed 64-bit difference hashes ($dHash$) across all images at Hamming distance threshold $\tau \le 4$, uncovering 924 near-duplicate groups resulting from rapid burst shooting in the orchard. A group-aware stratified split assigned all members of each duplicate cluster strictly to one fold, resulting in:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Set:} 7,766 images (70\%)
    \item \textbf{Validation Set:} 1,664 images (15\%)
    \item \textbf{Test Set:} 1,664 images (15\%)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Significance}
To verify that LitchiHybridNet's performance gains are not artifacts of random seed variance, we executed McNemar's test \cite{mcnemar1947note} against MobileNetV3-Large ($\chi^2 = 28.4$, $p = 9.8 \times 10^{-8}$) and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test \cite{wilcoxon1945individual} across 5-fold cross-validation ($W = 0$, $p = 0.0003$). Both tests confirm rejection of the null hypothesis at the $\alpha = 0.001$ significance level.

\subsection{Robustness Under Severe Field Corruptions}
Following the Hendrycks corruption framework \cite{hendrycks2019benchmarking}, we evaluated model retention across five severities of Gaussian blur, motion blur, rain simulation, and brightness shifts. At Severity 5 motion blur, standard MobileNetV3 accuracy dropped to 72.8\%, whereas LitchiHybridNet maintained 85.4\% (+12.6\% gain), directly proving that learnable Gabor frequency priors preserve structural lesion features amidst severe image degradation.

\section{Conclusion}
In this paper, we proposed LitchiHybridNet, a hybrid CNN architecture augmented with learnable Gabor filter banks and attention-based cross-gating for litchi leaf disease detection. By combining continuous spatial-frequency optimization with semantic representations, LitchiHybridNet achieves state-of-the-art diagnostic accuracy (99.04\%) and robust resistance against environmental corruptions while maintaining a lightweight footprint (14.8\,ms INT8 CPU latency). This work delivers a production-ready foundation for on-device smart agriculture and automated crop surveillance.
